## Slide 2: The problem: a per-GB tax on compliance

**Layout:** Three columns, one icon each: ₹ (cost), puzzle piece (parsers), shield (regulation).

**On screen:**

- 100 GB/day at Sentinel pay-as-you-go ≈ ₹1.38 Cr per year ($4.30/GB, ₹88/$)
- Every new device needs a hand-written parser (Grok, regex, decoders)
- CERT-In: 180-day log retention in India, 6-hour incident reporting · DPDP: up to ₹250 Cr penalty · BSA s.63: hash-value certificates for electronic evidence

**Speaker notes:** Logs grow faster than budgets. The cheapest compliant answer today is to drop data, and that is the one thing regulators no longer allow.

**Evidence if asked:** Microsoft Sentinel pricing (third-party reported rate); CERT-In Directions 28-Apr-2022; DPDP Rules 2025

## Slide 3: The solution: one pipeline in front of everything

**Layout:** The architecture diagram from Part 1 §3.1, simplified to five boxes: inputs → journal → workers → buffers → outputs.

**On screen:**

- 15 input types → SHA-256 journal (the lossless archive) → parse, normalise (100-field schema) and enrich → 12 output types
- Keep 100 % locally; forward only selected events, byte for byte (format: raw)
- No loss under failure: kill -9, sink outage and full-buffer tests pass

**Speaker notes:** ULPF sits between devices and the SIEM. Everything is kept and sealed locally, and only what matters goes upstream.

**Evidence if asked:** ulpf/runtime/*, tests/test_chaos.py

## Slide 6: ROI: a 100 GB/day fintech

**Layout:** Waterfall chart: SIEM bill before → after → ULPF licence → net saving.

**On screen:**

## Slide 8: Competition

**Layout:** 2×2: x = per-GB vs flat pricing, y = cloud-only vs offline-capable. ULPF top-right.

**On screen:**

- Splunk, QRadar, Sentinel, Chronicle: detection depth and scale; priced per GB or by workload
- Elastic, Wazuh: free, but 16–64 GB per indexer node; Wazuh has the endpoint agent we lack
- Cribl, Vector: mature pipelines with faster per-core throughput; no learned parsers or evidence chain
- DNIF, Vehere (India): SIEM/SOAR and NDR; ULPF feeds them rather than replacing them

**Speaker notes:** We complement SIEMs by sitting in front of them. Where we lose today: detection content (Sigma comes in Q1), certifications, and raw speed per core.

**Evidence if asked:** Part 2 §2 (cited sources)
